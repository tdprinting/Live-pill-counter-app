# Pill database: data sources and provenance

Generated by `tools/build_pill_db.py` (drug lists in `tools/drug_lists.py`), output `src/pill-db.js`.
Pulled on **2026-10-18** (DailyMed database published 2026-10-16). Raw downloads are cached in `tools/cache/`
(git-ignored, about 250 MB gzip); re-running the script uses the cache, so it is fast and polite.

## Sources actually used

| Source | Endpoints | What we take | Terms |
|---|---|---|---|
| **DailyMed** (US National Library of Medicine), primary | `https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/services/v2/spls.json?drug_name=...&pagesize=100&page=N` (label list, paginated) and `.../spls/{setid}.xml` (the structured product label) | per tablet/capsule: SPLCOLOR, SPLSHAPE, SPLSIZE (mm), SPLIMPRINT, product name, ingredients and strengths, dosage form, labeler, NDC product code | Public US-government data. Appearance values are written by the manufacturer / repackager. No licence fee, no key. |
| **RxNav / RxNorm** (NLM) | `https://rxnav.nlm.nih.gov/REST/rxcui.json?name=...&search=1` and `/REST/rxcui/{id}/related.json?tty=BN` | RxNorm ingredient id (`rxcui`) per drug; brand names (BN) of each ingredient, used as a whitelist that decides whether a product name counts as a brand | Public data, free, no key. RxNorm content is NLM-maintained; some source vocabularies inside the full UMLS are licensed, but the RxNorm brand/ingredient names used here are the public RxNorm subset. |
| **openFDA** (FDA) | `https://api.fda.gov/drug/ndc.json?search=product_ndc:"..."+OR+...&limit=100` (40 product codes per request, throttled to under 240 requests per minute, no key) | brand name / generic name / product type per NDC product code. Only used to CONFIRM a prescription brand RxNav does not list. | Public domain / CC0 (see https://open.fda.gov/license/ and /terms/). The disclaimer applies: not for medical decisions. |

No commercial site (drugs.com, WebMD, RxList, etc.) was contacted. All requests: at most 6 in flight, retry with exponential backoff,
`User-Agent: pill-counter-db-builder/2.0`.

## Endpoints checked and NOT usable

- `rximage.nlm.nih.gov` (RxImage) and `pillbox.nlm.nih.gov` (Pillbox): both retired by NLM. Connection fails (HTTP 000) from here. Skipped.
- `api.fda.gov/drug/label.json` is reachable but has no appearance fields, so it was not needed (names already come from DailyMed + NDC).

## Counts (this build)

- Queries sent to DailyMed: **754** (462 generic names + 288 brand names; 4 junk entries were removed after the first run and returned nothing anyway). 63,240 label listings found; per query at most 25 labels with
  distinct labelers were chosen (manufacturers first, then up to 8 repackagers, preferring labels whose title is exactly that drug rather than a combination).
  Roughly 10,000 label XML files were downloaded and parsed (9,594 unique setids with pill data).
- 18,286 raw product rows (one per tablet/capsule product per label) -> **11,185 distinct appearances** after dedupe on
  (ingredients, strength, imprint normalised, colours, shape, size within 1 mm, immediate vs extended release). Merged rows keep up to 6 labelers.
- 589 distinct ingredient sets ("drugs"), about 2,000 distinct drug + strength combinations, about 1,000 distinct labelers.
- RxNav: 589 ingredient groups looked up, 549 resolved to an rxcui. openFDA: about 18,280 product codes asked, 14,663 found.
- Brand names: about 1,000 rows carry a brand (product name is an RxNav brand of the ingredient, or openFDA confirms a prescription brand).
- Size of `src/pill-db.js`: **0.83 MB** (was 0.33 MB with 1,257 rows / 148 drugs).

## Row format in pill-db.js

`export const PILL_DB = { v:2, built, cols, S, L, C, H, R }`. `S` is an interned string table, `L` labelers, `C` colour combinations, `H` shapes.
Each row in `R` is `[name, generic, strength, imprint, color, shape, size*10, labelers[], brand, ndc, rxcui, form, n]`
where `name/generic/strength/brand/form` are indexes into `S`, `color` into `C`, `shape` into `H`, `labelers` into `L`;
`imprint` is the raw SPL text (sides separated by `;`), `ndc` the NDC product code (labeler-product), `rxcui` the RxNorm ingredient id,
`n` how many source labels share this exact appearance.

## Cleaning rules (so nobody is surprised)

- Only products with a colour AND an imprint are kept (no imprint = nothing to confirm). Size comes from the label (always present in this data).
- Products with more than 5 active ingredients (multivitamins) are dropped.
- Strengths: single spaces, `ug` shown as `mcg`; a bare unit `1` (sloppy label, e.g. "250 1") becomes `mg` only if another label of the same
  ingredient states that amount in mg, otherwise the unit is left off.
- Salt words (hydrochloride, sodium, besylate, ...) are ignored when deciding two products are the same drug.
- Names: ALL-CAPS product names are converted to title case.

## Known gaps and limits

- DailyMed appearance text is entered by each labeler. Mistakes exist (for example a repackager's label listing the wrong imprint under a brand name). We do not correct them.
- At most 25 labelers per drug were sampled (a drug like atorvastatin has about 400 labels). Real tablets from labelers we did not sample will not match exactly;
  the identifier is tuned to also work "by look-alike" (see `test/ID-RESULTS.md`).
- 56 of the 754 queries returned no pill data: discontinued or not-listed brands (Pravachol, Prinivil, Toprol-XL, Glucophage, Zofran, Flexeril, Mobic, Naprosyn, Vicodin, Subutex and others),
  kits (norgestimate/ethinyl estradiol, drospirenone/ethinyl estradiol, Yaz, Ortho Tri-Cyclen). Their generics are covered through the generic queries.
- Oral contraceptive blister packs, multivitamins and anything dispensed as a kit are not in the data (their SPL imprint data is on the kit, not the tablet).
- Pills with several colours listed in one product are stored with at most the colours the label gives (two-tone capsules fine; 3-colour products use the first two in matching).
- No images. (NLM RxImage / Pillbox are retired.)
